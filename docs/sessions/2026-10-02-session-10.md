## The three findings worth recording

### The reference images

The blocker above. Recorded here rather than smoothed over, because the report would otherwise
imply the screenshots were used.

### Two real bugs the existing harness caught

Both were introduced by this redesign and both were found by `responsive:check`, not by looking at
the page:

1. **Settings was unreachable on mobile.** The rail footer held it, and the rail is
   `display: none` below 1024px — so at 320px through 768px there was no navigation to the page at
   all. A page nothing can navigate to is not a page.
2. **The mobile navigation scrolled instead of wrapping.** Phase 6 hit this exact decision and
   chose wrapping; this redesign had quietly undone it. A link scrolled off the right edge is a
   link that does not exist as far as the person using it is concerned.

Both are fixed. Both would have shipped if the harness had not been run.

### Three date-coupling bugs in the existing test suite

`routine:test`, `skills:test`, and `regression:test` began failing on 2026-10-02 and passed the
day before. They are **not** caused by this redesign — confirmed by `git stash` and re-running on
the pre-redesign tree, where they fail identically.

The cause is a class of test bug rather than a product bug: a fixture writing to a hard-coded date,
and a read that either defaulted to the real UTC date or named a different hard-coded day. They
were correct on the day they were written and wrong on the next.

Fixed by making each read name the day it actually wrote, or by deriving the day from the clock
the route used:

- `skills-test.mjs` — `readDashboard()` → `readDashboard("2026-10-01")`.
- `routine-test.mjs` — the night check-in writes *tomorrow*, so the read derives tomorrow.
- `regression-test.mjs` — the photo route resolves `day: "today"` from the real clock, so `TODAY`
  is the real UTC day rather than a literal.

These fixes make the assertions *more* precise. No assertion was weakened, removed, or loosened.

## Backend preservation

**No file under `src/domain`, `src/lib`, `src/commands`, or `src/features` was modified.** Verified
by inspecting `git status`.

The Dashboard composes two extra read models — `readSkills` for the skills card — directly in the
page, the way it already imported the feature views, rather than by widening `readDashboard`. That
was a deliberate choice: the brief asked for a skills card, and the cheapest correct way to get one
is to read the existing model, not to add a field to a shared read model and change its contract.

## Three things the brief asked for that were not built, and why

1. **A recent-photos card on the Dashboard.** The diary is read in exactly one place and `npm run
   lint` refuses any other reader of it, naming the Dashboard specifically (ADR-061). The card is a
   link with no photograph, no note, and no count — a count would still be a statement about rows
   that page has no business holding.
2. **`role="progressbar"` on the Routine completion ring.** The Routine acceptance suite forbids
   both `<progress>` and `role="progressbar"` on that page, because a progress bar over a person's
   day is one step from scoring them. The ring is drawn and marked decorative; the same figure is
   stated in the sentence beside it.
3. **Account balances on the Dashboard.** `dashboard:accept` asserts the Dashboard does *not*
   contain "Balances" or "Bank 1" — today's spend and an account balance are different numbers and
   putting them on one card is the most common way a money screen misleads. The Dashboard reports
   the day's spend only.
